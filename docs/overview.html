How it works

Every recommendation follows the same pipeline. Funds are only ever compared with peers in their own category.

1 · Your mix"40% short term fixed income, 20% growth…" matched to 17 categories
→
2 · Prices2 years of dividend-adjusted daily closes; Yahoo, with Tiingo as backup
→
3 · Metrics1/3/12-month return, return vs category median, risk-adjusted ratio
→
4 · RankPercentile score within the category, 0 to 1
→
5 · BuildFour sample portfolios, backtested against a category-average baseline

Universe

113 ETFs in 17 categories (short-term bonds, AAA CLOs, Treasuries, commodities, growth, defensive, dividend, international…) plus 104 retail Fidelity mutual funds. Editable JSON files.

Risk-free rate

T-bills (BIL) over the same period. "Excess return" means return above BIL.

Outputs

Markdown reports for reading, or --json for other tools. Prices are cached per day.

Scoring

For each horizon, an ETF earns a blend of two peer percentiles, then horizons are averaged.

score = Σ horizon_weight × (
          risk_weight       × rank(risk-adjusted ratio)
        + (1 − risk_weight) × rank(return) )

The measure also breaks ties between equally good Fidelity matches. Under Sortino or Calmar, a pick with no return above T-bills gets 0% weight, which can concentrate a category in one fund.

Each portfolio is backtested over 12 months against the baseline. The backtest uses the same window the ETFs were chosen on, so it overstates what they will do next. Treat it as a sanity check, not a forecast.

Fidelity mutual fund equivalents

For any ETF, find the Fidelity mutual fund that has moved most like it over the last 12 months.

  • Tracking error ranks candidates: the annualized volatility of the daily return difference. Unlike correlation, it penalizes a fund that moves the same way but twice as far.
  • Match rating: close tracking error ≤ 2% or correlation ≥ 0.95; fair ≤ 6% or ≥ 0.80; loose Fidelity has no real equivalent.
  • With --sortino or --calmar, the higher-ratio fund wins among equally good matches. A better performer never wins by being a worse match.
  • Money market funds are excluded: their fixed $1 price carries no tracking information.

Daily portfolio monitor

Describe what you own in portfolio.json; the monitor checks it and proposes trades only when a rule fires.

Drift: the 5/25 rule

A holding more than 5 percentage points or more than 25% of its target away from target. A 10% target at 13% triggers (3 points is 30% of 10%).

Persistent laggard

Bottom half of its category at each of the last 4 weekly checks, and the best unheld ETF ahead by ≥ 0.25 every time. One bad week never triggers a swap.

Safety checks

Missing or stale prices (more than 4 days old) block every suggestion. Suggestions only: you place trades yourself.

Portfolio file

{ "account_type": "tax_deferred",
  "cash": 100000, "cash_target": 21.2,
  "holdings": [
    {"ticker": "FDFAX", "shares": 2084, "target": 45.08,
     "category": "defensive_equity"},
    {"ticker": "FBGRX", "shares": 602,  "target": 33.72,
     "category": "growth_equity"} ] }
  • Targets plus cash_target add up to 100. Cash follows the same 5/25 rule; with no cash target, cash is invested once it passes 5%.
  • Account type: tax_deferred (IRA, 401k) ignores taxes; taxable adds capital-gains and wash-sale warnings.
  • Import from a Fidelity positions CSV or a TICKER SHARES list. Mutual funds are placed in the category of the ETF they track most closely; money market funds (…XX) count as cash.
  • All rule thresholds can be overridden in the file.

Usage

Everything runs from one command. Add --json to any of them for machine-readable output.

Suggest and compare

python3 -m etf_agent "40% short term fixed income,
  10% senior AAA, 10% commodities, 20% growth,
  20% defensive"
python3 -m etf_agent --sortino --fidelity \
  "60% growth, 40% short term"
python3 -m etf_agent --list-categories

Find Fidelity equivalents

python3 -m etf_agent --match XLP QQQ SGOV

Set up and monitor a portfolio

python3 -m etf_agent \
  --import-holdings positions.csv \
  "40% short term, 10% AAA, 10% commodities,
   20% growth, 20% defensive" > portfolio.json
python3 -m etf_agent --monitor portfolio.json --sortino

# try the sample portfolio
python3 -m etf_agent --monitor examples/three_funds.json

In Amp

Ask in plain English in this repo, for example "suggest a conservative 60/40 portfolio with Fidelity funds". The suggesting-etf-portfolios skill runs the tool and summarizes.

Setup notes

Email and cron (any Linux machine with Python 3.10+)

# ~/.etf-agent.env  (chmod 600)
export SMTP_HOST=smtp.gmail.com SMTP_PORT=587
export SMTP_USER=you@gmail.com
export SMTP_PASSWORD='your-app-password'
export EMAIL_TO=you@gmail.com
export TIINGO_API_KEY=...   # optional backup data

# crontab -e: weekdays at 9 p.m., after fund prices post
0 21 * * 1-5 . $HOME/.etf-agent.env &&
  cd $HOME/EFT-Portfolio && python3 -m etf_agent
  --monitor portfolio.json --sortino --email

The cron entry is one line in practice (wrapped here). Gmail needs an app password (2-Step Verification on). Cron uses the machine's time zone.

Data sources

  • Yahoo Finance (primary): free, unofficial, no published limits; may throttle cloud servers.
  • Tiingo (backup, when TIINGO_API_KEY is set): official API with ETF and mutual fund prices; free plan 50 requests/hour, 1,000/day, 500 tickers/month.
  • Not usable: Google Finance (no API; Sheets can't export history or mutual funds), Microsoft (MSN undocumented, Bing APIs retired Aug 2025), Fidelity (no public retail API).

Privacy

portfolio.json is git-ignored. Never commit it, even once: git keeps tracking a committed file, and the GitHub repo is public. Samples live in examples/.

Caveats

  • Past performance: rankings use the last 1, 3 and 12 months; backtests are in-sample. Neither predicts future returns.
  • Concentration: Sortino and Calmar weighting can put most or all of a category in one fund. Calmar's drawdown floor limits but doesn't remove this for near-cash funds.
  • Data: Yahoo is unofficial and can change or block without notice. A bad data day produces "no action", never a trade.
  • Replacements come from the category's ETF list; a 401(k) menu may not offer them. Use --match for a Fidelity equivalent.
  • Taxes: taxable accounts get warnings only; lot-level cost basis isn't tracked.
  • Not yet tested live: Tiingo (no key), real SMTP delivery (tested with a simulated mail server), a real Fidelity CSV export (format written from documentation and memory), Fidelity research links (fidelity.com blocks automated checks).
  • Hand-picked lists: the ETF and Fidelity fund lists are curated, not exhaustive; edit the JSON files to change them.
  • Suggestions only. It never places trades. Not investment advice.
